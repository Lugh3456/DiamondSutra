第十七章 · 究竟无我分

金刚般若波罗蜜经

尔时，须菩提白佛言：「世尊！善男子、善女人，发阿耨多罗三藐三菩提心，云何应住？云何降伏其心？」佛告须菩提：「善男子、善女人，发阿耨多罗三藐三菩提心者，当生如是心：我应灭度一切众生。灭度一切众生已，而无有一众生实灭度者。何以故？须菩提！若菩萨有我相、人相、众生相、寿者相，则非菩萨。所以者何？须菩提！实无有法，发阿耨多罗三藐三菩提心者。须菩提！于意云何？如来于燃灯佛所，有法得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提不？不也，世尊！如我解佛所说义，佛于燃灯佛所，无有法得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提。佛言：如是如是。须菩提！实无有法，如来得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提。须菩提！若有法，如来得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提者，燃灯佛即不与我授记：汝于来世，当得作佛，号释迦牟尼。以实无有法，得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提，是故燃灯佛与我授记，作是言：汝于来世，当得作佛，号释迦牟尼。何以故？如来者，即诸法如义。若有人言：如来得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提，须菩提！实无有法，佛得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提。须菩提！如来所得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提，于是中无实无虚。是故如来说：一切法皆是佛法。须菩提！所言一切法者，即非一切法，是故名一切法。须菩提！譬如人身长大。须菩提言：世尊！如来说人身长大，则为非大身，是名大身。须菩提！如来说有我者，则非有我，而凡夫之人以为有我。须菩提！凡夫者，如来说则非凡夫，是名凡夫。」

逐句解释

尔时，须菩提白佛言：「世尊！善男子、善女人，发阿耨多罗三藐三菩提心，云何应住？云何降伏其心

这是第三章的核心教义的重述和深化：菩萨应当发心「我要度尽一切众生」——同时内心清楚地知道「实际上没有一个众生被度了」。这两句话同时成立，没有矛盾。前者是悲愿，后者是智慧。悲与智的合一，才是真正的菩萨行。

I shall liberate all beings — yet in truth not a single being is liberated

This paradox is restated here more fully than in Chapter 3, and now we have more context to understand it. The bodhisattva vow and the wisdom of emptiness are not in conflict — they are two aspects of the same awakened mind. Compassion (karuna) generates the limitless will to help; wisdom (prajna) sees through the illusion of a helper, beings helped, and the act of helping. Both must be present. Compassion without wisdom becomes ego-driven rescue. Wisdom without compassion becomes cold detachment. Together they are the Mahayana path.

灭度一切众生已，而无有一众生实灭度者。

度尽一切众生，而无一众生实被度——悲愿与智慧同时圆满，才是菩萨行。

Having liberated all beings, yet no being has been liberated — compassion and wisdom simultaneously complete

The Mahayana paradox restated directly. The bodhisattva does not stop at the intention to liberate all beings — they also dissolve the conception of beings to be liberated and a liberator who liberates. Without the vow, practice becomes self-absorbed; without dissolving it into emptiness, practice becomes ego-driven.

何以故？须菩提！若菩萨有我相、人相、众生相、寿者相，则非菩萨。

菩萨若有四相执着即非菩萨。在第十七章，连「菩萨」身份本身也不能执着。

A bodhisattva with the four marks is not a bodhisattva — including the mark of "being a bodhisattva"

Chapter 17 returns to the four marks with greater depth than Chapter 3. By now the sutra has spent chapters deconstructing identity, attainment, and teaching. The reminder carries all that weight: we are not just talking about avoiding self-centred giving, but the complete dissolution of any fixed vantage point.

所以者何？须菩提！实无有法，发阿耨多罗三藐三菩提心者。

没有一个固定的「法」或「方法」叫做「发菩提心」。菩提心不是一个可以被程序化、被制度化的东西，不是按步骤做了某件事就算发了菩提心。它是一种真实的内在觉醒——超越了所有固定的形式和方法。

In truth, there is no fixed dharma called 'arousing the aspiration for enlightenment'

Bodhicitta — the aspiration for enlightenment for all beings — cannot be reduced to a formula, a ceremony, or a checklist. There is no technique that, when correctly performed, 'counts' as having generated bodhicitta. It is a genuine inner movement, arising naturally when the mind is clear and the heart is open. You cannot manufacture it by following instructions. This does not mean it cannot be cultivated — but cultivation here means removing obstacles to what is already present, not constructing something new.

须菩提！于意云何？如来于燃灯佛所，有法得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提不？不也，世尊！如我解佛所说义，佛于燃灯佛所，无有法得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提。

佛陀问须菩提：我在燃灯佛处，有没有通过某种「法」而得到无上正觉？须菩提回答：没有。觉悟不是通过获得某种法而实现的——执着于法的人不可能成佛。燃灯佛是释迦牟尼过去世的授记之师。

At Dipankara Buddha's presence, was there a dharma through which the Tathagata attained awakening? No.

Dipankara (燃灯佛) was the ancient Buddha who prophesied Shakyamuni's future Buddhahood. The question cuts to the heart: was there some specific teaching or dharma-object that produced the awakening? Subhuti says no. Awakening is not produced by acquiring something — if it were, that acquiring would be a trace of ego, and the very ego that awakening dissolves. Even the historical Buddha's own path was free from any 'obtaining.'

佛言：如是如是。须菩提！实无有法，如来得阴耳多罗三藐三菩提。

佛陀双重肯定：就是这样。实无有法，如来得阴耳多罗三藐三菩提。觉悟不是执行的某种程序的结果。

The Buddha confirms: exactly so — there is no fixed dharma through which the Tathagata attained enlightenment

The double affirmation carries the full weight of the teaching. The point is not that enlightenment is unreal, but that no methodology causes it. Enlightenment is not the result of performing a particular sequence of practices correctly. It is what remains when all conceptual overlays — including "methods to attain enlightenment" — have dissolved.

须菩提！若有法，如来得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提者，燃灯佛即不与我授记：汝于来世，当得作佛，号释迦牟尼。

如果有某种「法」是如来用来得无上正觉的，那么燃灯佛就不会给我授记。正是因为没有这样的法，授记才能成立。执着于「法」的人不可能成佛；真正的授记，是给那个没有任何执着的人的。

If there were a dharma through which I attained awakening, Dipankara would not have given the prophecy

The logic is precise: a prophecy of Buddhahood is only valid for someone in whom no ego-grasping remains — including no grasping at the dharma itself. If there had been a specific thing Shakyamuni obtained, that obtaining would be a trace of ego, voiding the prophecy. The absence of any graspable dharma is exactly what made the prediction possible. The prophecy rests on emptiness, not on achievement.

以实无有法，得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提，是故燃灯佛与我授记，作是言：汝于来世，当得作佛，号释迦牟尼。

正是因为「实无有法可得」，燃灯佛才给我授记：你于来世当得作佛，号释迦牟尼。这是第十七章的核心肯定——授记的基础，不是某种成就，而是对「无成就可得」的彻底领悟。

Because there is truly no dharma to obtain, Dipankara gave the prophecy: in a future life you will become a Buddha named Shakyamuni

The positive statement of the chapter's central paradox. The prophecy of Buddhahood rests on emptiness, not achievement. Shakyamuni received the prediction precisely because there was no residue of 'obtaining' in him. This is the complete inversion of ordinary spiritual ambition: the person who seeks nothing, grasps nothing, and claims nothing is the one who inherits everything. Not as a reward — but because that is what awakening is.

何以故？如来者，即诸法如义。

「如来」的含义：如其本来而来，与诸法的本性完全相契，不增不减。

The Tathagata is so called because of his accordance with the true nature of all phenomena — thusness

"Tathagata" (如来) means "thus-come" — the one who comes and goes in perfect accord with thusness (如), the unmodified, self-evident reality of all things. His presence is not an additional thing on top of reality; it is reality fully expressed. The Tathagata's enlightenment is not an achievement — it is a complete transparency to what is already so.

若有人言：如来得阴耳多罗三藐三菩提，须菩提！实无有法，佛得阴耳多罗三藐三菩提。

「得」菩提隐含了获得者与被得对象，而觉悟不是一个可以被「得」的东西。

To say the Tathagata "attained" enlightenment subtly imports subject-object structure — in truth, there is no dharma the Buddha attained

Language about "attaining" enlightenment implies a self that gains an object previously absent. But enlightenment is not an object separate from the enlightened mind — it is the natural clarity of mind itself when no longer obscured by grasping. "Attaining" it is like "attaining" space: the space was never absent, nothing was actually added.

须菩提！如来所得阿耨多罗三藐三菩提，于是中无实无虚。

如来所得菩提无实无虚——既非固定实在，也非空无一物，超越有无二元。

In the enlightenment of the Tathagata, there is neither something real nor something empty

"Neither real nor empty" (无实无虚) refuses both substantialism and nihilism. It is one of the sutra's most precise philosophical statements. Enlightenment as the natural openness of awareness cannot be called "a thing" or "nothing." The middle path here is not a compromise but a different mode of understanding altogether.

是故如来说：一切法皆是佛法。

因此如来说：一切法皆是佛法。佛法不是特殊规定，而是对万法本性的如实认识。

Therefore the Tathagata says: all dharmas are buddha-dharmas

Having established that enlightenment is neither a fixed thing nor nothing, the sutra draws a sweeping consequence: every phenomenon is a buddha-dharma. Not just "spiritual" things — everything. This does not mean all distinctions collapse; it means the fundamental nature of all things — arising and passing without fixed essence — is already the nature of enlightenment.

须菩提！所言一切法者，即非一切法，是故名一切法。

「一切法」也被解构——即非一切法，是名一切法。连最宽广的教义也不能成为教条。

"All dharmas" is not a fixed category — it is called "all dharmas"

The sutra applies its signature deconstruction to its own statement. "All dharmas are buddha-dharmas" is immediately qualified: "all dharmas" is not a fixed boundary to be grasped. Even the most liberating insight must be held lightly. The sutra makes a statement and immediately prevents you from turning it into a dogma.

须菩提！譬如人身长大。

过渡句：佛以身长大为例，须菩提立即接话，师徒默契的象征。

Suppose a man's body were very large — a transitional sentence Subhuti immediately completes

In the Diamond Sutra's dialogical style, the Buddha sometimes speaks only the premise and Subhuti completes the argument — a sign of deep absorption of the teaching. This brief sentence sets up the next point by analogy. The "large body" will immediately be deconstructed.

须菩提言：世尊！如来说人身长大，则为非大身，是名大身。

须菩提接话：如来说身长大，则为非大身，是名大身。「大」无固定实质，只是相对的描述。

Subhuti completes: what the Tathagata calls "a large body" is not a large body — it is called that

Subhuti completes the exchange without the Buddha spelling it out — a mark of genuine understanding. "Large" is not an intrinsic property; it is a description relative to other things. The "is-not-A" move strips "large" of any fixed essence. This is not a denial that some things are larger than others — it is a refusal to let size become an independent reality.

须菩提！如来说有我者，则非有我，而凡夫之人以为有我。

「我」被如来说为「非有我」，凡夫执为有实上的我，这是苦的根源。

What the Tathagata calls "self" is not self — yet ordinary people take it to be self

The sutra makes a precise distinction: the Tathagata uses "self" as a conventional label for the continuity of experience. But ordinary people mistake the label for a fixed entity — defending, protecting, and mourning a self that was never the kind of thing that could be defended or lost. This mistake is the root of suffering.

须菩提！凡夫者，如来说则非凡夫，是名凡夫。

凡夫不是固定身份，每人都有佛性，觉悟对所有人始终开放。

"Ordinary person" is not a fixed state — it is called "ordinary person" and the gate is always open

The deconstruction of "ordinary person" is quietly radical. If being ordinary were essential and fixed, liberation would be impossible for ordinary people. But the sutra refuses this. "Ordinary person" describes a temporary condition — of not yet having seen through the illusion of self. The condition is not permanent. The capacity for awakening is present in everyone.

须菩提再次提出第二章的核心问题：云何应住？云何降伏其心？

第十七章回到了第二章的那两个核心问题——心应如何安住？如何降伏？这不是重复，而是深化：经过前面十五章的展开，现在佛陀给出更完整的回答。这个结构说明《金刚经》是一个螺旋式深入的教法，每次重问，都在更深的层次上回答。

Subhuti asks again: how should the mind abide? How should it be subdued?

The sutra returns to its opening question from Chapter 2, and this is deliberate. The Diamond Sutra is structured as a spiral: the same question recurs, but each time it is asked, the teaching has gone deeper and the answer can go further. In Chapter 2, the question opened the teaching. Here in Chapter 17, it invites the sutra's fullest articulation of what 'no-self' actually means in practice — not as a philosophical proposition, but as a lived reality.

总结 · Summary

第十七章回到了第二章的核心问题，给出更深的回答：菩萨发心度一切众生，同时知道没有一个众生被度——悲与智的合一。发菩提心没有固定的方法，因为它是一种真实的内在觉醒，不是程序。最后：「我」不是固定的实体，「凡夫」也不是固定的身份——没有人被永久锁定在任何状态中。觉悟的可能性，对所有人永远开放。

Chapter 17 returns to the sutra's opening question and provides its deepest answer: compassion and wisdom are not opposites but two aspects of the same awakened mind — vow to liberate all beings while knowing no being is ultimately liberated. Bodhicitta has no fixed method because it is a genuine inner awakening, not a procedure. And the chapter ends by dissolving both 'self' and 'ordinary person' as fixed categories — no one is permanently locked into their current state. The gate is always open.
